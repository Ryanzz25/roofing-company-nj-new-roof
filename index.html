Roof Installation in New Jersey: A Checklist for Additions, Dormers and New Builds

A new roof has no old roof to learn from, so the details have to be planned before anything goes on the deck. Work through this list with the contractor before the first bundle goes up.

Is the deck ready?

Walk the sheathing with the roofer. Gaps between panels, soft spots or a nail pattern that looks off should be pointed out and fixed before shingling, not after. Rain on bare sheathing or in an unfinished attic does damage fast, so ask how the roof will be dried in if weather moves in mid-job.

Do you know the order of the layers?

Ask the roofer to explain the sequence: deck check, drip edge, synthetic underlayment, ice and water shield at the eaves and valleys, step and counter flashing at any wall, shingles, then ridge vent. If the answer is vague, ask again.

How does the addition tie in?

Additions are where new roofs most often go wrong. The new slope should match the existing pitch without leaving a flat spot or a valley that holds water. Step flashing where the addition meets the old wall should be woven into the shingle courses, not caulked on afterward, and a continuous strip of ice and water shield along that joint helps prevent the slow leak that otherwise appears the first winter.

Will the ventilation carry across?

Ask how attic airflow will run across both the old and new sections, so one corner does not end up hot and damp. Soffit intake paired with ridge exhaust, sized to the attic, is the usual answer.

Will the shingles match?

Current product lines can usually match color and profile closely. If the original shingles are discontinued or heavily weathered, ask to see the closest options before anything is ordered.

Are the gutters part of the plan?

Hanging gutters while the roof goes on lets the drip edge and gutter line up cleanly; see gutter installation in NJ for sizing questions.

Is the paperwork in order?

For new construction and additions, the roof usually falls under the permit for the larger project, decided by the town's construction office. Ask for the contractor's NJ Home Improvement Contractor registration, an itemized written estimate, and what they put in writing about workmanship and the manufacturer's coverage.
